🎓 AI-Powered Phishing Email Detection System

An advanced, privacy-first cybersecurity system developed for academic evaluation and real-world triage. It combines deterministic pattern heuristics with on-premise Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning via Ollama to detect zero-day spear-phishing, credential theft, and social engineering attacks without transmitting sensitive email content to third-party cloud APIs.

🔍 5 Core Rule-Based Threat Indicators (0–60 Points)

1. Suspicious URLs (Max 15 pts)

Identifies raw IP hosts (bypassing DNS), URL shorteners concealing destinations, high-risk TLDs (.xyz, .top, .tk), deceptive subdomains, and embedded '@' credential tricks.

2. Urgent / Coercive Language (Max 10 pts)

Flags psychological manipulation tactics such as imminent account termination threats, 24-hour ultimatums, fake legal summons, or fabricated security breach notifications.

3. Password & OTP Harvesting (Max 15 pts)

Intercepts direct credential solicitation, password resets, PIN prompts, and one-time password (OTP/2FA) extraction. Legitimate service providers never request OTPs via email.

4. Financial & Payment Fraud (Max 10 pts)

Detects untraceable cryptocurrency wallet addresses, gift card purchases (iTunes/Amazon), payroll direct-deposit redirect scams, and unsolicited overdue invoice alerts.

5. Sender Spoofing & Domain Mismatches (Max 10 pts)

Exposes Display Name spoofing (e.g. "PayPal Support" sent from a generic gmail.com or unrelated domain), brand typosquatting (e.g. micros0ft.com, paypa1.com), and anomalous disposable mail sender infrastructure.

🎯 College Viva Voce Defense Guide (Common Questions & Answers)

Q1: Why use a hybrid approach (Rules + AI) instead of AI alone?

Answer: Standalone LLMs can hallucinate, suffer from non-deterministic variance, and can be bypassed by adversarial prompt injection or character obfuscation. Deterministic rule engines provide instant, reproducible, and verifiable evidence for known IoCs (Indicators of Compromise). Meanwhile, the LLM provides semantic reasoning to identify subtle social engineering tactics that rigid regex patterns miss. Combining both yields high precision and explainability.

Q2: What is the difference between AI Confidence and Threat Risk Score?

Answer: Risk Score (0–100) measures how dangerous and malicious the email is based on threat signals. AI Confidence (0–100%) measures the statistical certainty of the model regarding its classification. A routine university newsletter has a Risk Score of 0, but the AI may have 99% confidence that it is benign.

Q3: How does the application handle Ollama server failure?

Answer: The architecture implements graceful degradation. If Ollama is offline or times out, the application never crashes. The system catches connection errors, notes that AI is offline, and normalizes the deterministic rule score (0–60 scaled to 0–100) with an advisory warning in the UI.

Q4: Why run Ollama locally instead of OpenAI or cloud APIs?

Answer: Privacy and compliance. Corporate and personal emails contain confidential intellectual property, personally identifiable information (PII), and financial records. Transmitting email contents to third-party cloud APIs violates strict privacy policies (GDPR, HIPAA). Ollama runs 100% on the local workstation with zero telemetry.
